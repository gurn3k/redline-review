# Agent 3: Existing Tools

## Products

### 1. Pact (usepact.org)
- **What it does:** iOS app that takes an uploaded contract (lease, NDA, freelance agreement, vendor deal) and returns a clause-by-clause risk assessment (high/medium/low severity), plain-English explanations, suggested alternative/negotiation language, and document/version comparison, typically in under 60 seconds. Also has an "AI Lawyer" Q&A feature.
- **Target audience:** Renters, freelancers, small business owners, and employees reviewing employment contracts/offer letters — i.e., individual consumers and small operators, not law firms.
- **Pricing:** Token-based, no subscription: 5 tokens for $4.99, 10 tokens for $7.99 ("best value"), 20 tokens for $12.99; 1 token = 1 question or 1 contract analysis. Tokens don't expire. (Source: usepact.org)
- **Most common complaint:** Self-disclosed limitations rather than third-party reviews were the only sourced complaints found: it explicitly states it is "legal information, not legal advice," recommends users redact PII before uploading (data-security caution), and is iOS-only with Android only "on the roadmap." No independent user reviews (App Store/Trustpilot) were found within the search budget.
- **Source URL(s):** https://www.usepact.org/ ; https://www.usepact.org/blog/post/best-ai-contract-review-tools-freelancers-small-business

This is the closest existing analog to Redline's concept (severity-ranked risky clauses, plain English, drafted counter-language), but is mobile-only and lacks a document-grounded Q&A box and a full plain-English "summary" product framing.

### 2. ContractClarifyAI
- **What it does:** Web tool — paste or upload a contract and get red flags, a numeric risk score, and plain-English explanations of problem clauses, delivered in about 60 seconds.
- **Target audience:** Freelancers and small businesses who want contract review without hiring a lawyer.
- **Pricing:** Free tier (no signup required) for basic analysis; also referenced as $9 one-time or $29/month subscription in a separate aggregator listing.
- **Most common complaint:** No user reviews or complaints were found on the pages checked; the only caveat surfaced is the site's own disclaimer that it "is not a law firm and does not provide legal advice."
- **Source URL(s):** https://contractclarifyai.com/blog/best-ai-contract-review-tools-freelancers ; https://alternativeto.net/software/contractclarifyai/about

### 3. ClauseGuard
- **What it does:** Scans a freelance contract in ~30 seconds and returns a 0–100 risk score, flagged clauses, plain-language explanations, and ready-to-use negotiation language; keeps an analysis history.
- **Target audience:** Explicitly "freelance designers, developers, writers, consultants, and small agencies who sign contracts regularly but can't afford a lawyer for every deal."
- **Pricing:** Freemium — 3 free analyses without a credit card, then a paid subscription for unlimited/full functionality (exact price not found).
- **Most common complaint:** Not found — the AlternativeTo listing had zero reviews ("No comments or reviews, maybe you want to be first?").
- **Source URL(s):** https://alternativeto.net/software/clauseguard/about/

### 4. Terms of Service; Didn't Read (ToS;DR)
- **What it does:** Free, open-source, community-curated browser extension (Chrome/Firefox/Edge/Safari/Opera) that grades a website's Terms of Service / privacy policy from A (best) to E (worst) and shows a short list of good/bad/blocker points via an address-bar icon.
- **Target audience:** General consumers who want a fast read on any website's ToS before agreeing.
- **Pricing:** Free, open source.
- **Most common complaint:** Two recurring gripes in reviews: (1) it doesn't cover every website (coverage is limited to sites the volunteer curator community has rated), and (2) discomfort with the browser permissions it requests ("not sure why you got to comb through my browser history like that though"). Chrome Web Store rating is 4.6/5 from 133 reviews.
- **Source URL(s):** https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/terms-of-service-didn%E2%80%99t-r/hjdoplcnndgiblooccencgcggcoihigg/reviews ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terms_of_Service;_Didn%27t_Read ; https://github.com/tosdr/browser-extensions

### 5. DoNotPay
- **What it does:** Consumer app marketed as "the world's first robot lawyer" — subscription cancellation, chargebacks/refunds, parking-ticket appeals, and other automated consumer-legal tasks (not a contract-clause analyzer per se, but the best-known "AI does your legal paperwork" consumer brand and a useful cautionary comparison).
- **Target audience:** General consumers.
- **Pricing:** ~$36 billed every two months (roughly $216/year); an AI Chat add-on at $10/month.
- **Most common complaint:** Billing complaints dominate — being charged twice or continuing to be billed after cancellation attempts (one reviewer reported "charged twice per month at $36 each — totaling $72 per month" for nearly a year); advertised features not working as described; slow/absent customer support. Trustpilot TrustScore is 1.8/5 from 383 reviews. The company also settled with the FTC in September 2024, paying $193,000 for allegedly overstating what its "robot lawyer" could actually do (order finalized January 2025).
- **Source URL(s):** https://www.trustpilot.com/review/donotpay.com ; https://www.technewsworld.com/story/robot-lawyer-faces-legal-troubles-of-its-own-86956.html ; https://technologylaw.fkks.com/post/102j901/donotpay-cases-underscore-hurdles-for-ai-fueled-legal-help

### 6. Spellbook
- **What it does:** AI contract review/drafting platform built as a Microsoft Word add-in for lawyers — redlines contracts against organizational playbooks/standards, drafts clauses from precedent, answers contract questions with citations, and benchmarks agreements against market norms.
- **Target audience:** In-house legal teams and law firms (referenced customers include Dropbox, Panasonic, eBay, ASICS); "4,500+ legal teams" across 80+ countries. Not aimed at individual consumers.
- **Pricing:** No public pricing disclosed on the main site (7-day free trial offered; pricing "scoped through conversation"). Third-party aggregators report a wide, inconsistent range — one source cites Pro ~$20/mo and Team ~$40/user/mo, while another cites Starter $99/user/mo and Professional $149/user/mo — so treat exact figures as unverified.
- **Most common complaint:** Not directly sourced from reviews (G2/TrustRadius pages were not fully accessible within the read budget); one secondary source noted "limited public review presence." No independently verified complaint text found.
- **Source URL(s):** https://spellbook.com/ ; https://www.hyperstart.com/blog/spellbook-pricing/

### 7. LawGeex
- **What it does:** Contract review automation platform — reviews and redlines incoming business contracts (NDAs, services agreements, SaaS agreements) against predefined company policies/playbooks and reports compliance issues automatically.
- **Target audience:** In-house legal teams at enterprises and mid-size companies triaging high volumes of routine contracts (site lists enterprise, mid-size, small business, and freelancer segments, but coverage skews to legal/compliance/procurement teams at larger orgs; referenced customers include eBay and HP).
- **Pricing:** Not disclosed publicly ("contact vendor"); no free trial listed on Capterra. One estimate from a third-party guide put costs at "several thousand USD annually" for midsize teams, unverified.
- **Most common complaint:** Direct user reviews were not available on the platforms checked — Capterra and GetApp both showed zero reviews for LawGeex at the time of this search. The clearest recurring friction point found was pricing opacity: reviewers/aggregators repeatedly note "no general pricing available" and recommend requesting a demo/quote before evaluating.
- **Source URL(s):** https://www.capterra.com/p/170181/LawGeex/ ; https://www.getapp.com/operations-management-software/a/lawgeex/ ; https://ai.g2.com/marketplace/tools/lawgeex

### 8. Robin AI
- **What it does:** AI legal platform for contract review, redlining, and search — flags risks, generates red-flag reports, and assists with contract negotiation/drafting.
- **Target audience:** Business/legal teams (in-house legal and commercial teams at companies), not individual consumers.
- **Pricing:** Not disclosed on the primary site; third-party estimates vary widely and are inconsistent — one source cites plans starting near $5,000/year with enterprise deployments at $40,000–$80,000, another cites $30,000–$50,000/year overall. Treat as unverified/directional only.
- **Most common complaint:** AI accuracy/trust issues — one review noted Robin AI "often misunderstands the phrasing of legal theory," and more broadly reviewers flagged risk of AI hallucinations on complex contracts and the continued need for human oversight before relying on its output.
- **Source URL(s):** https://www.layer3labs.io/guides/robin-ai-explained ; https://www.staymodern.ai/solutions/robin-ai-contract-review/detailed

## Patterns across tools

Across both the consumer/freelancer tools (Pact, ContractClarifyAI, ClauseGuard) and the enterprise legal tools (Spellbook, LawGeex, Robin AI), pricing transparency is a recurring weak spot — most sites either hide pricing behind "contact vendor"/demo requests or third-party estimates conflict wildly with each other, making it hard for a buyer to comparison-shop. On the consumer side specifically, the closest analogs to Redline (Pact, ContractClarifyAI, ClauseGuard) are new enough that they had little to no independent review coverage (App Store/Trustpilot/G2/AlternativeTo) at the time of this search, so their real-world complaint patterns are not yet established in public sources — the only sourced friction points were self-disclosed disclaimers (not legal advice, redact PII, platform limitations) rather than user complaints.

## What I could not find

- Independent user reviews (App Store, Trustpilot, G2, Reddit/HN threads) for Pact, ContractClarifyAI, and ClauseGuard — these are close analogs to Redline but appear too new or too small to have accumulated public review text within this search.
- Verified, first-party pricing for Spellbook, LawGeex, and Robin AI — all three keep pricing off their marketing sites; only conflicting third-party estimates were found, and none could be confirmed against an official source.
- A sourced, specific complaint for Spellbook and LawGeex — G2/Capterra/GetApp pages either blocked direct fetch (403) or showed zero reviews at the time of checking.
- Any Reddit or Hacker News discussion threads specifically about these tools — search results surfaced aggregator/SEO content and vendor comparison pages rather than organic forum discussion.
- App Store-specific reviews for Pact (iOS-only) — a generic web search returned unrelated apps with similar names (a parental-control app and a fitness-rewards app both also called "Pact") rather than the contract-review app's own listing.
